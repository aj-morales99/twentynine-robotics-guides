# Wheels & gears check-up

This guide starts with the wheels. A gearbox inspection section will be added after the procedure and acceptable movement are verified.

<div class="know-fact know-fact--danger" role="note">
  <span class="know-fact__label">Before touching the wheels</span>
  <p>Power off the robot, disconnect its battery when accessible, and keep the weapon system disabled. Place the robot on a stable stand so it cannot drive away.</p>
</div>

## What you need

- The Allen key supplied with the Mini Hunter kit
- The spare wheel set screws supplied with the kit
- Optional removable, medium-strength threadlocker
- A clean tray so a small set screw cannot roll away

The wheel uses a removable, squared red silicone tire around a black **3D-printed ABS inner rim**. The inner rim incorporates a metal flange coupling that locks onto the motor shaft with set screws. Access holes around the rim's outer circumference let the Allen key reach those screws after the silicone tire is removed.

The illustrated assembly is approximately **50 mm in diameter** and **30 mm wide**, with a **10 mm-thick red silicone tire**. The JGA25-370 motor remains secured in its mount while part of the gearbox is exposed and the flange coupling sits slightly inside the rim.

## 1 Check for a loose wheel

1. Keep the powered-off robot stationary on its stand.
2. Hold one wheel and rotate it gently.
3. Rock it very lightly along the motor shaft—toward and away from the robot and from side to side.
4. Watch the point where the black wheel rim meets the motor shaft.

Some movement inside a geared motor can feel like normal gear play. The wheel needs attention when the **rim itself slides, wobbles, or turns loosely on the shaft**.

<div class="know-fact know-fact--success" role="note">
  <span class="know-fact__label">A secure wheel</span>
  <p>The wheel remains centered on the shaft, does not slide in or out, and turns without rubbing the motor mount or robot body.</p>
</div>

## 2 Remove only the silicone tire

1. Hold the black ABS rim steady without pulling it away from the motor.
2. Grip the edge of the red silicone tire.
3. Stretch and slide the silicone tire evenly off the outside of the rim.
4. Leave the black ABS rim, metal flange coupling, motor shaft, gearbox, and motor mount assembled.

Do not pry against the motor or gearbox. Work around the tire a little at a time if it does not slide off evenly.

## 3 Find a rim access hole

With the red silicone tire removed, inspect the outer circumference of the black ABS rim. Several holes are distributed around this surface. Slowly rotate the rim by hand until one access hole lines up with a flange-coupling set screw.

Insert the supplied Allen key through the selected hole and make sure it seats fully in the set-screw socket before turning it.

## 4 Inspect and tighten the set screw

Check that the access hole is clear and the set screw still accepts the Allen key firmly. Turn the key gently **to the right (clockwise)** until the flange coupling is secure on the shaft. Stop when the rim no longer slips; do not force the key after the screw is seated.

If the motor shaft has a flat side, make sure the set screw tightens against that flat. Replace the set screw with the supplied spare if its socket is rounded, its threads are damaged, or the Allen key can no longer grip it correctly. Continuing with a damaged screw can leave it stuck inside the coupling.

If the Allen key tip is visibly rounded or twisted, replace the key before tightening another screw.

If you are replacing the set screw or adding threadlocker:

1. Turn the set screw **to the left (counterclockwise)** and remove it carefully through the access hole.
2. Apply only a **very small amount of removable, medium-strength threadlocker** to the screw threads.
3. Reinsert the screw through the same access hole.
4. Turn it gently **to the right (clockwise)** until secure.

Keep threadlocker away from the motor, gearbox, bearings, ABS rim, and silicone tire.

<div class="know-fact know-fact--danger" role="note">
  <span class="know-fact__label">Do not overtighten</span>
  <p>Too much force can round the set-screw socket, damage its threads, or damage the Allen key tip. Replace damaged hardware instead of forcing it.</p>
</div>

## 5 Reinstall the silicone tire and check both wheels

Stretch the red silicone tire evenly over the black ABS rim. Work around its circumference until it sits flat and centered. Repeat the same inspection on the other wheel, even if only one wheel originally felt loose.

After both wheels are installed:

1. Turn each wheel by hand and confirm it remains centered.
2. Check that neither wheel slides along the shaft or rubs the mount.
3. Confirm both set screws are present and their access holes are clear.
4. Perform the first powered wheel test with the robot supported and the weapon disabled.

<div class="know-fact know-fact--success" role="note">
  <span class="know-fact__label">Ready for the next check</span>
  <p>Both wheels should remain secure, rotate without rubbing, and respond evenly during the supported low-speed test.</p>
</div>
